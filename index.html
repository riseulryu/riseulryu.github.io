About

My research is in artificial intelligence, with published work in multimodal biometric authentication. My doctoral thesis addressed how authentication systems can adapt their operations in response to changing context, device and user conditions. The underlying position is that a person's biometric data is not a fixed template but a distribution that changes with time, context and device, so static models degrade as that distribution drifts.

My current project is a systematic review of how explainable AI contributes to cybersecurity and sustainability. I designed the search strategy across four databases, screened retrieved records against explicit inclusion criteria, and am applying a quality assessment instrument to the included studies.

I am also developing a second line of work from the same observation. Biometric data also changes with mental state, which raises the question of whether facial and keystroke signals can serve as indicators for detecting and monitoring mental health conditions.

Alongside this I have seven years of university teaching across three Australian institutions, in programming, cybersecurity, web development and network administration. My published record spans two strands rather than one: security and authentication, and computing education research.

My background is cross-disciplinary. Before moving into information technology I completed a Master of Professional Accounting and a Bachelor of Economics, and worked for three years as an accountant at Air Liquide Korea, where I ran an ERP migration to Oracle and validated financial data integrity across business units. That grounding in systems, data assurance and organisational process informs how I frame security research: not as an isolated technical problem, but as one embedded in the practices of the people and organisations that rely on it.
